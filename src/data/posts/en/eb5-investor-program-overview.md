The EB-5 Immigrant Investor Program offers a direct path to a U.S. green card for foreign nationals who invest in a new commercial enterprise that creates jobs for American workers. Created by Congress in 1990, the program has become one of the most sought-after immigration pathways for high-net-worth individuals and families from China, Hong Kong, and beyond.

## How the Program Works

Investors must commit capital to a qualifying U.S. business venture. The investment must create or preserve at least 10 full-time jobs for qualifying U.S. workers. Upon satisfying these requirements, the investor, their spouse, and unmarried children under 21 can all obtain lawful permanent resident status (a green card).

## Investment Thresholds

The required investment amount depends on where the project is located:

- **$1,050,000** for projects in standard areas
- **$800,000** for projects in Targeted Employment Areas (TEAs) — rural areas or areas with high unemployment

These amounts were updated by the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022 and are subject to periodic adjustment for inflation.

## Direct Investment vs. Regional Centers

Investors have two primary routes:

**Direct Investment** requires the investor to be directly involved in the day-to-day management or policy formulation of the business. This path suits entrepreneurs who want active control.

**Regional Center Investment** allows passive investment through USCIS-designated organizations that pool EB-5 capital for large-scale development projects such as hotels, mixed-use real estate, or infrastructure. Indirect job creation through economic modeling is permitted, making it easier to satisfy the 10-job requirement.

## The Application Process

1. **Select a project** — Conduct thorough due diligence on the commercial enterprise or regional center
2. **Transfer funds** — Place the investment capital "at risk" in the business
3. **File Form I-526E** (Regional Center) or **I-526** (Direct) — USCIS adjudicates the petition
4. **Apply for immigrant visa or adjustment of status** — Once approved, investors abroad file DS-260; those already in the U.S. file I-485
5. **Receive conditional green card** — Valid for two years
6. **File Form I-829** — Remove conditions on permanent residence within 90 days before the two-year anniversary
7. **Receive unconditional green card** — Full permanent resident status granted

## Key Considerations

**Source of Funds**: USCIS requires comprehensive documentation proving that your investment capital was lawfully obtained. This includes tax returns, business records, sale agreements, inheritance documents, or gift letters — all translated into English and supported by certified records.

**Retrogression**: Demand from mainland Chinese investors has historically been so high that visa numbers can become backlogged. Work with your attorney to understand the current visa bulletin and projected wait times for your country of birth.

**Project Risk**: Unlike a bank deposit, EB-5 capital is genuinely "at risk." Investors may lose some or all of their investment if the project fails. Always perform independent financial and legal due diligence before committing funds.
