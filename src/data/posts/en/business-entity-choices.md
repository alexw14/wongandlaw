Choosing the right legal structure is one of the most consequential early decisions for any business owner. The entity type you select determines how your business is taxed, how much personal liability protection you receive, and how easily you can bring in investors or partners later. This article compares the three most common choices for U.S. businesses: the LLC, the S-Corporation, and the C-Corporation.

## Limited Liability Company (LLC)

An LLC is the most flexible and commonly chosen structure for small to mid-sized businesses. Key attributes include:

- **Pass-through taxation**: Profits and losses flow directly to members' personal tax returns, avoiding the "double taxation" associated with C-Corps
- **Limited liability**: Members are generally not personally responsible for business debts or lawsuits
- **Flexible management**: Can be member-managed or manager-managed, with operating agreement terms largely customizable
- **Minimal formalities**: No requirement for a board of directors, annual meetings, or complex record-keeping

LLCs are ideal for real estate holdings, professional practices, consulting firms, and family businesses. Foreign nationals can hold membership interests in an LLC, making it a popular choice for international entrepreneurs entering the U.S. market.

## S-Corporation

An S-Corp is a pass-through entity that avoids double taxation while offering one significant tax advantage over LLCs: **self-employment tax savings**. Owners who work in the business can split their income between a "reasonable salary" (subject to payroll taxes) and distributions (not subject to self-employment tax).

However, S-Corps come with strict eligibility requirements:

- Maximum of **100 shareholders**
- Shareholders must be **U.S. citizens or permanent residents** — foreign nationals cannot hold S-Corp stock
- Only **one class of stock** is permitted
- Certain entity types (other corporations, LLCs, partnerships) cannot be shareholders

The citizenship restriction is a critical limitation for non-resident alien investors and international founders.

## C-Corporation

A C-Corp is the preferred structure when you plan to seek venture capital, issue multiple classes of stock, or eventually pursue an IPO. Notable features include:

- **Unlimited shareholders** with no citizenship restrictions — foreign nationals and entities can invest freely
- **Multiple classes of stock** (common, preferred, etc.) suited for complex equity arrangements
- **Fringe benefits**: Certain employee benefits are deductible at the corporate level
- **Retained earnings**: Profits can be retained in the company at the corporate tax rate (currently 21%) rather than flowing to shareholders immediately

The main downside is **double taxation**: the corporation pays income tax on profits, and shareholders pay tax again on dividends received. For early-stage startups that reinvest all profits, this is often not a practical concern.

## Which Structure Is Right for You?

| Factor | LLC | S-Corp | C-Corp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pass-through taxation | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| Foreign ownership allowed | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |
| Self-employment tax savings | Limited | ✓ | N/A |
| VC / investor-friendly | Limited | Limited | ✓ |
| Formality requirements | Low | Moderate | High |

For a Chinese or Hong Kong entrepreneur establishing a U.S. business, the **LLC** or **C-Corp** are almost always the right choices. An LLC works well for operating businesses where the owner will actively manage the company and prefers simplicity. A C-Corp is the right choice when external investment, stock option plans, or long-term exit strategies are a priority.

## Next Steps

Before forming an entity, consult with both a business attorney and a CPA who understands cross-border tax implications. The right structure today can save significant taxes and legal complications down the road.
